Arrival gap and actual waiting time

Two friends arrive independently and uniformly within an hour. Let D = |TA − TB| be their arrival gap and W = min(D, a) the first friend's actual wait. Change the waiting allowance a; the arrival model stays fixed.

A CDF reports P(variable ≤ t). Use the two threshold buttons to see why the value at the allowance includes the whole point mass.

Gap CDF: FD(t)

For 0 ≤ t ≤ 1 hour, FD(t) = 2t − t². There is no jump.

Actual-wait CDF: FW(t)

Open circle: excluded left-limit value. Filled circle: actual CDF value. The dashed connector indicates a jump; its interior is not part of the CDF.

PDF and continuous component

Dashed grey: gap PDF. Solid teal: W's continuous component, zero above the allowance. Its area is . Endpoint density values do not affect probabilities.

Point mass at W = a

The mass is a probability, not a density height. For 0 < a < 60 minutes, W has a mixed law and no ordinary PDF for its entire distribution.

Read the units and the complete law

Charts use minutes. The gap density is (2/60)(1 − m/60) per minute for 0 < m < 60. The formulas below use a in hours.

P(W = a) = (1 − a)²; E[W] = a − a² + a³/3;
E[W²] = a² − 4a³/3 + a⁴/2; Var(W) = E[W²] − E[W]².

At a = 0, all probability is at zero. At a = 60 minutes, W = D and the atom disappears. Moving the threshold changes the event being measured, not the distribution.
