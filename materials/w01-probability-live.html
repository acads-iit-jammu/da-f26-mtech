A ticket-routing review

A service desk checks whether an LLM routes tickets to the appropriate teams. A reviewer supplies reference decisions; the model supplies classifications. What would it mean for them to agree?

Introduce the task and the two sources of labels before displaying the table.

Suggested working:

We will compare the reviewer reference outcome with the model classification ticket by ticket. The example is hypothetical. The reviewer decisions are operational references, not claims of error-free ground truth.

For this hypothetical audit, the model uses a 0.50 threshold separately on each destination.

What does the table count as an agreement?

Clarify that Other means neither named destination is required; another team may handle the ticket. The reference decisions are operational, not asserted ground truth.

Suggested working:

For t1t_1, the model scores are 0.55 for Billing and 0.35 for Account Support. The reviewer reference includes both destinations. Exact agreement means the model classification equals the full reviewer reference outcome.

Which rows agree?

Mark exact agreement between the reviewer reference and model classification. How many of the six records agree?

Ask for reasons on t1 and t4; do not yet call the fraction a future performance probability.

Suggested working:

Exact agreements occur on t2,t3,t5,t6t_2,t_3,t_5,t_6. Thus the observed agreement fraction is 4/6=2/34/6=2/3.

What does four out of six describe?

The table records four agreements. Is 4/64/6 a probability for every question about this model?

Distinguish a data summary from a probability statement by asking for the experiment.

Suggested working:

It is first an observed proportion in these six records. It becomes the probability of agreement if the experiment is to select one of these six ticket IDs uniformly. A claim about future tickets needs a target population and assumptions linking this audit to it.

Equal-likelihood counting

If one of the six ticket IDs is selected uniformly, what is the probability that the selected record shows agreement?

Give students time to set up the problem before discussing the result.

Suggested working:

The classical rule for a finite set of equally likely cases is P(E)=|E|/|Ω|P(E)=|E|/|\Omega|. Here each ID has probability 1/61/6 and four IDs show agreement, so P(agreement)=4/6=2/3P(\text{agreement})=4/6=2/3. The uniform selection rule justifies equal weights for IDs in this experiment.

The cases being counted matter

The occupied reviewer/model pairs are: both destinations / Billing (t1t_1); Billing only / Billing (t2,t5t_2,t_5); Account only / Account (t3,t6t_3,t_6); Other / Account (t4t_4). If one pair type were selected uniformly, what agreement probability would result?

Have students explain why changing the partition changes the sample unit and hence the selection rule.

Suggested working:

Two of four occupied types are agreements, so the answer under uniform selection among types is 2/4=1/22/4=1/2. It differs from 4/64/6 under uniform selection among ticket IDs because the categories contain unequal numbers of records.

A larger set of possible label pairs

There are four possible reviewer reference types and two model classes. How many pairs are possible in their full Cartesian product? How many appear here?

Separate the set of possible combinations from the subset observed in the audit.

Suggested working:

There are 4×2=84\times2=8 possible pairs in the full product. Four are occupied in this six-row table. Uniform weighting over eight pairs would be a third selection rule and is not implied by the audit.

Frequency and repeated observations

Does an agreement fraction of 4/64/6 establish a long-run agreement rate for future tickets?

Keep observed frequency, model probability and future estimation distinct.

Suggested working:

No. A frequency account connects probability to relative frequency in a specified repeatable process. If IiI_i indicates agreement on trial ii, the observed frequency after nn trials is n−1∑i=1nIin^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n I_i; a limiting account relates this sequence to a probability pp. Six observations alone establish no such limit and do not establish that future tickets follow the same process.

Belief and evidence

Suppose an analyst assigns probability 0.8 to agreement on the next ticket, given this audit and other information. Does the table alone determine 0.8?

State that these are interpretations, not competing arithmetic rules for the same fully specified experiment.

Suggested working:

No. A subjective account treats probability as a degree of belief given information. The assignment needs stated information or judgments and must be coherent with the analyst’s other probability assignments. An evidential account also needs a specified rule connecting evidence and background assumptions to a number.

What the earlier views contribute

What does each account emphasize: equal-likelihood counting, frequency, belief, or evidential support? What remains necessary for a calculation?

Use the slide as a transition, not a verdict on philosophical interpretations.

Suggested working:

The accounts emphasize symmetry, repeated observations, information-conditioned belief, and evidential support. A calculation still needs specified outcomes, an event and a probability assignment. A propensity interpretation instead locates chance in a specified physical or randomized mechanism.

A second question: a random chord

What must a probability model specify?

The ticket audit and the chord question both involve uncertainty. What mathematical objects would let us state the outcomes, the event and its probability?

Requirements on the event collection

If EE is an event, should “not EE” also be an event? If E1,E2,…E_1,E_2,\ldots are events, what about “at least one occurs”?

These are closure requirements on the collection of events. Keep them distinct from the probability axioms on the next slide.

Suggested working:

A sigma-algebra ℱ\mathcal F on Ω\Omega satisfies:

  1. Ω∈ℱ\Omega\in\mathcal F.
  2. If E∈ℱE\in\mathcal F, then Ec∈ℱE^c\in\mathcal F.
  3. If E1,E2,…∈ℱE_1,E_2,\ldots\in\mathcal F, then ⋃n=1∞En∈ℱ\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_n\in\mathcal F.

It therefore contains ⌀\varnothing too. In the finite ticket model, ℱ=2Ω\mathcal F=2^\Omega satisfies these conditions.

Kolmogorov’s probability axioms

What conditions should a probability assignment satisfy on events?

State nonnegativity, normalization and countable additivity separately from sigma-algebra closure.

Suggested working:

For P:ℱ→[0,1]P:\mathcal F\to[0,1]:

  1. Nonnegativity: P(E)≥0P(E)\ge0 for every E∈ℱE\in\mathcal F.
  2. Normalization: P(Ω)=1P(\Omega)=1.
  3. Countable additivity: for pairwise disjoint E1,E2,…∈ℱE_1,E_2,\ldots\in\mathcal F, P(⋃n=1∞En)=∑n=1∞P(En)P(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_n)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(E_n).

For example, BB and BcB^c are disjoint and exhaustive, and P(B)+P(Bc)=3/6+3/6=1P(B)+P(B^c)=3/6+3/6=1.

A continuous ticket-time question

Let TcT_c be the closure time, in hours, of a ticket from a specified process. Under an illustrative Gamma model with shape 2 and scale 12 hours, calculate P(Tc>24)P(T_c>24).

Read the units and parameterization aloud. Explain that shape 2 is not the mean; 24 hours is the mean under this shape-scale model.

Suggested working:

The model is stipulated for illustration, not estimated from the six-ticket audit. Its mean is αθ=2(12)=24\alpha\theta=2(12)=24 hours. The Gamma shape-scale density is f(t)=t144e−t/12f(t)=\frac{t}{144}e^{-t/12} for t≥0t\ge0. The required probability is the area under this density to the right of 24 hours.

From counting to integration

For the six equally likely ticket IDs, an event probability is found by counting IDs. For continuous closure time under this density, what replaces counting individual durations?

Connect the shaded area to the integral, not to the height of the curve.

Suggested working:

For a ticket-ID event CC, P(C)=|C|/6=∑ti∈CP({ti})P(C)=|C|/6=\sum_{t_i\in C}P(\{t_i\}). Under a continuous density model, probabilities are integrals over ranges. A density value is not itself a probability. The shaded right-tail area is P(Tc>24)=∫24∞t144e−t/12dtP(T_c>24)=\int_{24}^{\infty}\frac{t}{144}e^{-t/12}\,dt.
