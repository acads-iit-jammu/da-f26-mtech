What the central limit theorem says

For independent, identically distributed observations with finite positive variance, the standardized mean approaches a standard normal distribution as n grows. The original population does not become normal. The unstandardized averages become more concentrated around μ because their variance is σ²/n.

For normal input, the normal law of the mean is exact for every n. For rare Bernoulli events, the distribution remains discrete at every finite n: its visible spikes do not contradict convergence of its CDF to a continuous normal CDF. There is no universal sample size that guarantees an accurate approximation.
