When is a sum of Poisson counts Poisson?

Two counters record events over the same fixed interval. Start with independent Poisson counts, then introduce a shared component. The marginal laws stay Poisson, but dependence changes the distribution of their sum.

0% gives independence; increasing this creates positively correlated counts.

3. Inspect one probability by convolution

The model and the transform

N₁ = U + C; N₂ = V + C
U ~ Poisson(λ₁ − c), V ~ Poisson(λ₂ − c), C ~ Poisson(c), independently
c = shared fraction × min(λ₁, λ₂)

The sum is U + V + 2C: a shared event enters both counters and is counted twice. Cov(N₁, N₂) = c.

M of sum at t = exp[(λ₁ + λ₂ − 2c)(eᵗ − 1) + c(e²ᵗ − 1)]

At c = 0, this reduces to exp[(λ₁ + λ₂)(eᵗ − 1)], the MGF of Poisson(λ₁ + λ₂). When c > 0, multiplying the two marginal MGFs would incorrectly treat the counters as independent.
