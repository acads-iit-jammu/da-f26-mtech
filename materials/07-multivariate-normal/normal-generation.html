Lecture 01.7

Generate and whiten correlated normal measurements

Start with 700 fixed simulated independent standard-normal pairs. The same points are reused as correlation changes, making the effect of the transformation visible.

μ = (0, 0), σ₁ = 1, σ₂ = 2.

X₁ = Z₁; X₂ = 2(ρZ₁ + √(1−ρ²)Z₂).

Correlated output

Simulated correlated points.

After inverse transformation

Recovered simulated standard-normal points.

Z₁ = X₁; Z₂ = (X₂/2 − ρX₁)/√(1−ρ²).

Standardizing X₂ by dividing by 2 leaves correlation ρ. Whitening also removes the contribution shared with X₁. Gaussian whitening produces independent coordinates; for a general distribution, identity covariance alone establishes only uncorrelated coordinates.

The points are simulated using a fixed pseudorandom seed and the Box–Muller transform. Sample covariances use denominator n−1. Population statements follow from the model, not the finite scatterplot. A point outside a plot window is omitted visually but retained in the displayed statistics.
